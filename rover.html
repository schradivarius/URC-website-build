The machine

Shasta

A six-wheeled rover built to work where a person can't: 50 kilograms of chassis, arm, science lab and autonomy, driven from a kilometre away with nothing but a camera feed and a radio link.

Under the panels

Subsystems

Five subteams, one machine. Each group owns its subsystem end to end — requirements, design, fabrication, test and the field repairs at 3 a.m.

01Chassis & drivetrain

A welded aluminium frame on a passive rocker-bogie suspension that keeps all six wheels loaded over broken rock. Custom hubs, gearboxes and machined wheels give us clearance over obstacles taller than the wheels themselves, without a single actuator in the suspension to fail in the field.

SolidWorksFEA CNC & weldingDrivetrain design
02Robotic arm

Six degrees of freedom, roughly a metre of reach, and a gripper that has to be gentle enough for a keyboard and strong enough for a jerry can. The equipment servicing mission is won or lost here: every joint is designed around repeatable, camera-guided placement under teleoperation delay.

Inverse kinematicsHarmonic drives Closed-loop controlEnd effector design
03Science payload

An auger collects subsurface regolith into a sealed carousel, where onboard instruments measure moisture, pH and temperature and run a fluorescence assay for organic compounds. The results, plus site imagery and stratigraphy, become the evidence our science team defends in front of the judges.

Sample handlingSpectroscopy GeologyLab protocol
04Electronics & power

Custom PCBs handle motor control, power distribution and sensing off a 48 V battery, with fusing, e-stop and thermal margins sized for a desert afternoon. Harnessing is built to be serviceable — connectors labelled, runs documented, spares packed.

KiCadPower distribution Embedded CCAN bus
05Software & autonomy

A ROS 2 stack ties perception, localisation and planning together: fuse RTK GNSS with IMU and wheel odometry, detect the mission's fiducial markers and objects with onboard vision, and plan a path over terrain the operator can't see. The same stack drives the ground-station UI our drivers use for the other three missions.

Validation

Nothing ships untested

Utah is the worst possible place to discover a problem. So we find them at home: full mission rehearsals on Texas terrain, with the driver blind to the field and the clock running exactly as it will at competition.

  • Bench tests on every board before it touches the rover.
  • Drive-hour targets logged before each design freeze.
  • Timed, scored mission rehearsals with a real operations crew.
  • A written failure log — every anomaly root-caused before competition.
