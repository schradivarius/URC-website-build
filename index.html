Our mission

Build the rover that would keep astronauts alive.

The University Rover Challenge asks students to build the next generation of Mars rovers — machines capable of working alongside human crews on the surface of another world. Every design decision we make answers to that premise.

  • Every part is designed, machined, wired and coded by students.
  • Open to every major — engineering, computer science, geology, business and design.
  • Judged each June in the Utah desert against the best teams on the planet.

The competition

University Rover Challenge

Hosted by The Mars Society at the Mars Desert Research Station outside Hanksville, Utah. Roughly a hundred teams enter each year; a field of 38 finalists earns the trip to the desert.

Four missions, one rover

What the rover has to do

A single machine, under 50 kg, has to clear all four field missions in the Utah desert — with the operators sitting a kilometre away and out of sight.

Science

Collect and analyse soil samples in the field, then defend a hypothesis about past or present life to a panel of scientists.

Delivery

Find and ferry tools, containers and supplies across rough terrain to astronauts scattered around the site.

Equipment servicing

Operate a lander mock-up with the robotic arm: flip switches, turn valves, type on a keypad and insert a cache — all by camera.

Autonomous navigation

Drive to a series of GNSS waypoints and visual markers with no human input — no joystick, no teleoperation, no excuses.

The machine

Meet Shasta

A six-wheeled, rocker-bogie rover built for the red rock of the San Rafael Swell: a six-degree-of-freedom arm, an onboard science lab, and an autonomy stack that can find its own way to a waypoint.

Season

How a build year runs

  1. August — September

    Recruit & concept

    New members join subteams, last season's rover gets torn down, and we set the design goals for the year.

  2. October — December

    Design & prototype

    CAD, circuit boards, software architecture. Subsystems get prototyped and reviewed before anything is cut.

  3. January — March

    Build & System Acceptance Review

    Machining, wiring and integration, then the video and written report that decide which teams reach the finals.

  4. April — May

    Field testing

    Full mission rehearsals on Texas dirt: drive time, arm runs, autonomy trials, and failures found here instead of in Utah.

  5. Competition

    Mars Desert Research Station

    Four missions, three days, one rover — against the best student teams in the world.
