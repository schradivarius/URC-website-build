Our mission

Build the rover that would keep astronauts alive.

The University Rover Challenge asks students to build the next generation of Mars rovers — machines capable of working alongside human crews on the surface of another world. Every design decision we make answers to that premise.

  • Every part designed, machined, wired and coded by students.
  • Students from across engineering, computer science, geology, business and design.
  • Judged in the Utah desert against the best student teams in the world.

The competition

University Rover Challenge

Hosted by The Mars Society at the Mars Desert Research Station near Hanksville, Utah. Teams are scored on four field missions and a System Acceptance Review, 100 points each; only the highest-scoring teams at that review are invited to the desert.

Where
Hanksville, Utah — MDRS
Field competition
June 2–5, 2027
Down-select
Top 36 teams at the March review
Crew
Undergraduate & graduate students

Four missions, one rover

What the rover has to do

Four field missions across four days in the Utah desert — driven from a command station with no view of the course, up to a kilometre away.

Astrobiology

Choose sites in the field and analyse them with onboard instruments, then collect a sample from at least 10 cm down, seal it in a cache and defend the science to the judges.

Delivery

Find, pick up and deliver tools and containers of up to 5 kg to astronauts in the field, over stages of increasing difficulty.

Equipment servicing

Service a mock lander with a robotic arm: open drawers and panels, connect a hose, turn valves, and type a launch key on a keyboard autonomously.

Autonomy

Assist an astronaut on spoken and gestured commands — follow, stay, fetch — then find your own route to markers on hilly ground. No joystick, no teleoperation, no excuses.
